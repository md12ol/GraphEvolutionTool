How it works

Crossover & mutation

How children differ from their parents. Three probability rolls control it, all of them owned by the engine rather than by the genome — which is the design decision that makes the two settings mean the same thing in every configuration.

The three rolls

Crossover

Gated by crossover_rate, rolled once per pair. If it fires, the two parents recombine in place — leaving one child in each. Both children are kept, because crossover inherently produces two and an engine that kept only one would waste half of every crossover.

Each genome offers exactly one crossover operator, fixed by its representation:

GenomeOperator
edge-edit Two-point over genes. Draw a segment within the shorter genome's length and swap it. A genome shorter than two genes is left alone.
SDA Two-point over states. Swap a contiguous band of states — transitions and responses together. Swapping state 0 also swaps init_char, since together they determine the automaton's first transition.
There is no operator-selection setting, deliberately. Additional crossover operators are wanted, and they are explicitly out of scope until the rest of the design is delivered — which is a sequencing decision, not a design limit. Until a second operator exists there is nothing to select between, so no enum, no config field and no dispatch is built for it. When it lands it follows selection: one new variant plus one match arm, mapping onto a config field.

One quirk worth knowing under generational: parents are drawn with replacement, so the same individual can be picked twice. Crossing a genome with its own clone changes nothing, and the child differs only by mutation. Steady-state cannot self-mate by construction.

Mutation

Two independent rolls, and it is worth being precise about which does what:

SettingAnswersDefault
mutation_rateDoes this child mutate at all? required
max_mutations If it does, how many mutations does it take — drawn uniformly from 1..=max_mutations.1

So mutation_rate = 0.2, max_mutations = 4 means: 80% of children are untouched, and the other 20% take 1, 2, 3 or 4 mutations with equal probability. It does not mean each gene mutates with probability 0.2 — that is a different and much more common convention, and confusing the two will cost you a run.

Exactly one mutation per call

Genome::mutate applies exactly one mutation per call. This is a trait-level contract, not a per-representation choice. A genome that rolled its own count internally would make max_mutations mean nothing for that representation — and nothing would report the disagreement. The engine calls mutate in a loop; the genome does not get a vote on how many times.

What "one mutation" is belongs to the representation:

GenomeOne mutation is
edge-edit Reroll one gene — its opcode drawn from the configured operation mix, and its payload redrawn.
SDA Redraw either init_char (about 4% of calls), one transition target, or one response — even odds between the latter two.
The same count is not the same disruption across representations. One edge-edit mutation changes one edit in a script of gene_length. One SDA mutation changes one entry in a transition or response table — and because the automaton feeds on its own output, a single changed response can alter every character after it. SDA is correspondingly more disruptive at the same max_mutations.

A note for anyone porting old settings

Edge-edit previously rolled its own count, 1..=4, against a hardcoded constant. At the default max_mutations = 1 it now mutates less per event than it used to. Set max_mutations = 4 to recover the old strength. SDA is unchanged at the default.

Why the engine owns all of this

Both rolls happen in one shared helper, used by both strategies. That is not tidiness: selection sampling once drifted apart between the two strategies, and the mutation semantics are kept in one place specifically so the same thing cannot happen again. It also means a third strategy inherits correct variation behaviour for free — see Add a strategy.

Neither rate is range-checked. A mutation_rate of 1.5 or -0.1 parses and runs; see the warning on Python: TOML File.
